How to read this · data & methodology

Source. Actual historical daily minimum and maximum 2 m air temperature and daily total precipitation, downloaded from the Open-Meteo Historical Weather API on 30 October 2026. A fixed ERA5 model is used throughout 1991–2020, rather than a changing mix of weather products. ERA5 is a gridded reanalysis blending observations and a physical model, not a Madrid weather-station record. Requested location: central Madrid (40.4168, −3.7038); returned grid: 40.5, −3.75, reported elevation 666 m. Daily aggregates use Europe/Madrid time. Local streets, parks, elevation and urban heat can differ.

One calendar date = 450 historical days. For each date in February, pool that month/day and the seven dates on either side in every year from 1991 to 2020 (30 × 15). Dates are mapped to a non-leap calendar and 29 February is excluded, so each year has equal weight. For example, 1 February uses 25 January–8 February; 28 February uses 21 February–7 March. There are no missing values in these samples. Smoothing reduces the noise of using just 30 exact-date observations, but trades away date specificity. Adjacent date samples overlap heavily.

Temperature distributions. Calendar numbers are the separate medians of historical daily lows and daily highs. Ranges are empirical quantiles, using linear interpolation between sorted observations: middle 50% = 25th–75th percentiles; 80% = 10th–90th; 90% = 5th–95th. A middle 80% range leaves about one in five historical values outside it. Low and high distributions are marginal: their endpoints need not occur together. Calendar bar lengths share a fixed −12°C to 28°C axis (separate blue/rust half-tracks); the marker is the median. Histogram heights share a common scale across the two distributions for the selected date.

“Rain probability” means historical wet-day frequency. It is the fraction of sample days with at least 1.0 mm total precipitation, including rain and any snow water equivalent. It is not the chance of any drizzle, the fraction of the day that is wet, or the probability of rain throughout a visit. Snow alone can count as a wet day. Storms and neighboring dates are correlated; do not multiply these daily probabilities as if days were independent.

The three summary cards use all 840 non-leap February dates in the baseline (30 × 28), not the smoothed samples. The baseline is the standard 1991–2020 climate-normal period. It does not adjust for warming since 2020, future trends, or the specific circulation in February 2027. These probabilities assume historical conditions are a useful reference; they are not a calibrated 2027 predictive model. Use a short-range weather forecast close to departure.

Offline & reproducible. The relevant daily observations are embedded in this HTML. All calculations run locally, without tracking, external scripts or API calls. The download contains the currently selected range plus medians, wet-day counts and sample sizes for all 28 calendar dates.
