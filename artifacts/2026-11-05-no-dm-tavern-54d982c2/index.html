What you're trading away

You keep: fantasy characters, monsters, dice, loot, and character growth. Cooperative picks also keep the shared-party problem solving.

You give up: freeform storytelling, improvised NPC conversations, and the ability to try absolutely anything. These are board games with defined actions.

You still need: a rules teacher and someone to apply enemy rules. Scripted setup reduces handling; it does not turn TTS into a fully automated video game.

For six players: resolve routine turns briskly and spread rules duties around. Talisman and Runebound support six, but are weaker choices for a short evening.

Why not Gloomhaven, Descent, or the D&D Adventure System?

Gloomhaven normally seats four; Descent: Second Edition normally seats five including its overlord; the D&D Adventure System games normally cap at five. They miss this guide's requirement to support six without house rules. A good four-player game is not automatically a six-player recommendation.
